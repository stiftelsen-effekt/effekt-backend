Regranting Ledger
Design sketch · Gi Effektivt · 26 Oct 2026

Regranting from the database, not the sheet

A proposal for replacing the Tildelinger tab of Samlefila with a ledger in the admin panel. Donations already carry their split, so the system can work out what each organisation is owed. People then only decide what gets paid, when, and to whom.

What the sheet does today

I read the export back to 2018. It covers 35 recipient columns, about 35 transfer rounds, and roughly 38.9M kr received and 38.8M kr transferred in 2025. Each round repeats the same steps by hand:

MonthlySum per orgPaste monthly totals per recipient from the DB export (getMonthlyAggregateByOrganization).
RoundGjenstående til delingCarry-in plus inflow since the last round, per recipient.
RoundFaktisk overførtOne row per bank transfer: a GiveWell payment covers TCF, AGF, AMF, MC, HKI and NI.
RoundIkke overførtThe remainder, typed in as next round's carry-in.
Ad hocCorrectionsFees, “Korrigering forrige overføring”, major-donor rounds, fronted payments, #REF! cells.

The sheet is really a ledger, kept by hand. Most of the pain comes from the carry-forward step: one wrong cell flows into every later round, and the checksum column only catches some of those errors.

Core idea: one ledger per recipient

Every amount that changes what we owe a recipient becomes an entry in one table. A recipient's balance is the sum of its entries, so nothing needs to be carried forward by hand.

balance(org, t) = opening balance + Σ allocations − Σ transfer lines ± Σ adjustments (all entries dated ≤ t)

Allocations credit

One row per donation × organisation, from sum_confirmed × cause-area share × org share. They're read straight from the donation tables, so a correction made in the database flows through automatically.

Transfer lines debit

Money that has actually left the bank, split per organisation. One bank transfer to GiveWell becomes six lines.

Adjustments ±

Typed and signed entries with a reason and an author: fee, correction, reallocation, fronted amount, opening balance.

The database is the source of truth. Reconciling donations against the bank happens in the donation database, as it does today. The regranting system adds no reconciliation step of its own. If a donation is corrected after a round, the recipient's balance simply changes, and the next round pays the difference. Each approved round keeps a snapshot of the balances it was based on, so you can always see what a decision was made on.

A round, start to finish

1 · DraftPick a cutoffThe system proposes paying each recipient's balance at the cutoff, grouped by payee.
2 · EditAdjust amountsLower or hold amounts and add transfers. Anything not paid stays in the balance automatically.
3 · ApproveSecond person signs offThe round locks. The system produces payment instructions and grant letters per payee.
4 · PaidEnter actualsRecord the paid date, FX rate, foreign amount, bank fee and bank reference per transfer. The balances update straight away.

Fund grants don't have to follow the regular schedule. With Fund grants only, the round pays just the granted amounts, and AMF's and GiveDirectly's direct donations stay in their balances for the next regular round. With Regular + fund grants, both go out together. Either way, each recipient's Include direct donations switch can override the default. Try switching between the two.

In a real round the grant lines start empty. The team adds recipients, projects and amounts, and can choose any recipient in the system. A recipient can appear on several lines, one per project, plus one unrestricted. Whatever isn't granted stays in the fund for a later round. The grant and its rationale are approved together with the rest of the round.

Other screens

Balances

A table with one row per recipient: balance today, inflow since the last round, the last transfer, and a sparkline. It replaces scrolling to the bottom of the sheet.

Recipient statement

Every entry for one organisation over a period. Use it for grant letters, the annual report, and answering “how much did X get in 2025?”.

Round history

Past rounds with their balance snapshot, transfers and grant letters. It answers “what did we send in June, and why?”

Donation trace nice to have

From a donation ID: which allocations, which round, which transfer. This could later power “your money reached X on date Y” on Min Side.

Our own funds

Example: a donor gives 100 kr to the Gi Effektivt fund, and Gi Effektivt later decides that the fund pays 50% to AMF and 50% to GiveDirectly. The database already has a fund recipient for this (GEBF, GiEffektivts barnedødelighetsfond, currently inactive).

The fund is treated as a recipient that never receives a bank transfer. Its money leaves through fund grants: internal entries that move the balance from the fund to the final recipients. Money only reaches the bank in the normal rounds, from the AMF and GiveDirectly balances.

Don't expand at donation time

Rewriting the KID to 50/50 when the donation arrives would be simpler, but it loses the donor's actual choice. It also locks in today's split and leaves no room for reserves or later decisions. The donation stays 100% fund.

Decided in each round

A fund has no fixed split. In each round, the team chooses which recipients get how much of the fund's balance, writes a short rationale, and the round's approver signs off on it.

Tracing back to the donor nice to have

Not needed for regranting itself, and can be added later without changing the rest. Grants use fund money oldest-first (FIFO), pro rata across the donations in that slice, and the result is stored in Fund_grant_attributions. Min Side can then say “your 100 kr from March went 50 to AMF and 50 to GiveDirectly in June”. Before a grant it shows “in the fund, not yet granted”.

Counting without double counting

“Given to a charity” = direct allocations + fund grants. The fund's own inflow is reported as donor intent, and its unspent balance as a separate line with its age, so money waiting in the fund stays visible.

External funds such as GiveWell's All Grants Fund remain ordinary recipients. Our ledger stops where we hand money to a payee. A fund only needs this treatment when Gi Effektivt itself decides where the money goes. Fund-to-fund grants are blocked in the first version.

Projects

Some money goes to a recipient without restrictions, and some is tied to a specific project. A project is its own record in the database, belonging to one recipient. It holds the name, country, period, agreement reference and when reports are due.

  • Balances are kept per recipient and project. Donations go to the recipient's unrestricted balance. A fund grant to a project goes to that project's balance, so the round knows to pay it as a project line and not as unrestricted money.
  • A transfer can have several lines for the same recipient, e.g. one unrestricted line and two project lines in a single payment to GiveDirectly.
  • Projects can also be chosen directly in a round. The team can mark part of a recipient's unrestricted balance for a project when paying it, as in the August 2026 transfer “GiveDirectly prosjekt og Unrestricted”.

Project page

Everything sent to a project: transfers, dates, amounts, which fund grants paid for it, agreement and report deadlines. This is what an auditor or a due diligence review asks for.

Project report

Per project, or across all projects for a period: total granted, from which funds, and which reports are outstanding.

Migration

There's no need to make 2019–2025 match exactly. Pick a start date, take the sheet's balance at that date as truth, and let the database take over from there.

  • Cutoff: the end of the last completed round, e.g. 30 Sep 2026.
  • Opening balances: one opening adjustment per recipient, equal to the sheet's carry-over at the cutoff. Negative balances (MC, NI) carry over as they are.
  • History: optionally import the sheet's “faktisk overført” rows as historical transfers, so recipient statements go back to 2020. Mark them imported.
  • Shadow period: run one or two rounds in both systems, and diff the proposal against the sheet per recipient. Every difference becomes either a code fix or a documented adjustment.

Admin panel or a new product?

Recommendation: build it in the admin panel, backed by new routes and DAO modules in effekt-backend. Auth0 roles, the organisation list, and the donation and distribution queries are already there. The monthly-by-org SQL becomes the allocation generator almost unchanged. A separate product would need its own auth, database access and deploy pipeline, all to read the same tables.

Consider a separate service only if the system should be shared with Effektiv Spenden and other organisations later. Even then, the ledger tables and the round workflow above would stay the same, and only the allocation source would change. Giveon's grants table already plays a similar role on that side.

Suggested phases

  1. 1
    Allocations + balances (read-only)

    Add the Regrant_allocations view, load opening balances, and add a Balances page. Ops checks it against the sheet for one round.

  2. 2
    Rounds and transfers

    Round builder, payees, approval and recording actuals. Run it alongside the sheet for one regular round.

  3. 3
    Adjustments and the hard cases

    Fronted amounts, reallocations, fees, unassigned Facebook money, and projects on transfer lines and fund grants. Retire the sheet after this.

  4. 4
    Outputs

    Grant letters, payment files and recipient statements. Donation tracing on Min Side is a nice to have that can come after this.

Decisions for the team

  • Are fees taken out of the recipient's balance, or paid from operations? The sheet does both depending on the year.
  • Is a donation allocated at timestamp_confirmed or when it's registered? This matters for AvtaleGiro and late imports near a cutoff.
  • Does a round need a second approver, and who?
  • Should balances below a threshold (say 10 000 kr) be held automatically?
  • How far back should transfer history be imported: all of it, or only from 2024?
  • For our own funds: who approves grants, and how long can money stay in a fund before it must be granted?
  • Should recipients donors can pick today, such as GD MB, GDCSF or GD UNR, stay separate recipients or become projects under GiveDirectly? As projects, donors could no longer choose them unless the donation form learns about projects.
